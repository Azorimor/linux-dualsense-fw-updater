Supported Controller Hardware

Most Sony PlayStation DualSense controllers released after the 2020 launch work with this unofficial Linux firmware updater. Use the instant USB checker below to see right away whether your controller is supported.

Check Your Controller in the Browser

Plug in your DualSense or DualSense Edge via USB-C, then run a quick read-only check.

Runs 100% locally in your browser: read-only check, nothing is sent to any server and nothing on your controller is changed.

Supported Models at a Glance

Supported, Tested

Standard DualSense

Standard DualSense wireless controllers built after the 2020 launch window are fully supported and tested on Linux for official Sony firmware updates.

Supported, Untested

DualSense Edge

DualSense Edge firmware exists in the official Sony catalog, but flashing has not yet been tested on real DualSense Edge hardware. The app flags this clearly before flashing.

Not Supported

Original 2020 Launch DualSense

Earliest 2020 launch DualSense controllers (BDM-010) are not part of the official Sony PC update catalog. The app detects them and blocks updates.

What You Need

Wired USB-C Data Cable

Firmware updates can only run over a direct, data-capable USB-C cable. Bluetooth connections and power-only charging cables cannot flash controller firmware.

Automatic Detection

You do not need to open your controller or look up serial numbers. The Linux desktop app automatically identifies your board revision, fetches the matching Sony image, and prevents mismatched flashes.

Technical Reference

Show board revisions, USB IDs, and firmware targets

The updater reads HID Feature Report 0x20 ((hw_info >> 8) & 0xff and fw_series) together with the USB Product ID to map your controller to its official Sony firmware target. When permitted, Feature Reports 0x80 and 0x81 are used only to identify the controller shell colorway.
